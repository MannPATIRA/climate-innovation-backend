2Carbon13 builds and backs ventures with a key focus on addressing the climate emergency. 
Entrepreneurship is a powerful creative force in the economy and one that can spur 
industrial transformation. This is exactly what we need to deliver against net zero at speed 
and with innovation that unlocks new technological and commercial trajectories.
We bring founders together in a programme designed to catapult climatetech into the 
global companies of tomorrow. Moreover, Carbon13 fosters a community by connecting 
startups with experts and like-minded individuals. This network enables knowledge sharing, 
innovation, and collaboration, creating a positive social ecosystem which can accelerate 
the development of climatetech solutions. With our origins in Cambridge, we were able to 
leverage our existing connections within a world leading tech cluster.
This report is for our activities in 2021 and 2022, when we launched and developed our 
programme, and founders were selected to build ventures capable of mitigating 10 million 
tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalents (MtCO2e) per year when at scale. We secured funds 
which we deployed into promising ventures created in the venture builder programme and 
built out a wide stakeholder group. 
As a catalyst for problem solvers, Carbon13's impact on the environment, society, and 
the economy is substantial. While our primary impact remains focused on mitigating 
greenhouse gas emissions, we recognise the need to consider a broader range of impacts. 
We also acknowledge that how ventures are built matters. Founders have the opportunity 
to create the companies they wish existed and work inclusively with a broader range of 
perspectives which sparks new opportunities and ideas.
This is our first impact report and marks our commitment to sustainability and our mission 
to address climate change.Carbon13 Impact Report 2021/22

A significant

employees have at least

assumptions, home energy

to be lower than office

is that only two-thirds of 
employees working from 
home lead to heating-related 
emissions that wouldn't 
have already taken place (ie: 
incrementally). Combined

air-conditioning takes place

Tackling the Gender Gap: 
Elevating Gender Diversity 
as Our Primary Focus
As all aspects of Diversity, 
Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) 
are fundamental pillars of 
our organisation's ethos, 
we acknowledge their web 
of intersectionality and 
understand the complexity 
inherent in fostering an 
inclusive space. 
However, given our limited 
resources as a small 
organisation, we have 
strategically prioritised 
initiatives aimed at enhancing 
gender diversity. We 
recognise that mentoring, 
resources, networking, 
and funding opportunities 
that venture builders and 
accelerators offer can help 
address the equity gap.7 
Climate startups encounter 
specific hurdles, like long-
term return on investment and regulatory shifts, which 
accelerators can assist female 
entrepreneurs in navigating 
with valuable guidance and 
support. 
This segment of the impact 
report, thus, while underlining 
our commitment to a broad 
spectrum of social concerns, 
particularly emphasises 
our endeavours to reduce 
gender disparities in startup 
cofounding teams. 
This approach is based 
on the understanding that 
addressing gender inequality 
has a cascading effect, 
positively influencing various 
other social aspects, from 
economic participation to 
increasing innovative solutions 
for problems that impact us 
all. Investing more in female 
entrepreneurs has the 
potential to yield significant 
societal benefits, as research 
shows that women often 
allocate a higher portion of 
their income towards their 
families compared to men.8 
Female-founded businesses 
could also provide more job 
opportunities to women once 
startups with at least one 
female founder hire 2.5 times 
more women than male-
founded businesses.9 However, we are aware that 
our commitment to DEI 
extends beyond gender alone. 
As we progress, we remain 
dedicated to expanding our 
efforts to encompass broader 
diversity dimensions for a 
more comprehensive and 
equitable future. 
Despite proof that 
organisations with greater 
gender diversity at the 
governance and leadership 
level outperform the 
ones with less, female 
representation in startup 
founding teams ranges from 
around 10% to 20%. In 2022, 
women-founded companies 
received just 2% of VC 
investment, while only 15% 
of VC’s ‘cheque-writers’ were 
women. 21 Efforts to reshape 
this reality and enhance 
female representation 
in our programmes, and 
consequently in the startup 
world, remains a core focus. 
Across Cohorts 1 to 4, around 
25% of the participants were 
female, marking a positive 
step forward. However, we 
acknowledge the journey 
toward gender parity is 
extensive, and we have much 
more to do to reach a female 
critical mass in our cohorts.

Methodology
Assets and activities are 
assessed using carbon 
factors from government 
sources, reputable third 
party research, and xtonnes’ 
proprietary modelling, with 
the most relevant factors 
being applied to your 
business. Where proxy data 
exists for how much an asset 
or activity has been used or 
carried out, this creates a 
“modelled” footprint based on 
the organisation in question, 
giving the most likely, lower 
and upper case possibilities. 
Where data exists that 
measures the usage of 
an asset or completion of 
an activity, this creates a 
measure footprint, which 
typically receives an 
maximum quality score, and 
produces a single figure as 
opposed to a range.Glossary
Additionality: Additionality in 
climate-related matters refers 
to ensuring that claimed 
emission reductions are 
extra to what would naturally 
occur. This concept verifies 
that these claimed emission 
reductions wouldn't happen 
otherwise, emphasising 
their necessity in combating 
climate change.
Greenhouse Gas: 
Greenhouse gases 
(GHG) are gases that are 
responsible for the so-called 
greenhouse effect, which is 
considered the main cause 
of climate change. They can 
occur both naturally and 
anthropogenically (through 
man-made causes). 
Greenhouse Gas Protocol 
Corporate Accounting and 
Reporting Standards: GHGP 
(Greenhouse Gas Protocol) 
Corporate Accounting 
and Reporting Standard is 
traditionally considered the 
“gold standard” in carbon 
accounting methodologies. 
Developed with the World 
Resources Institute (WRI) 
and World Business Council for Sustainable Development 
(WBCSD), GHGP claims 
to provide the accounting 
platform for virtually every 
corporate GHG reporting 
program in the world. 
Carbon Footprint: The 
total of the greenhouse gas 
emissions (both direct and 
indirect) associated with 
an organisation, product or 
activity. 
Carbon Dioxide Emissions 
(CO2): Carbon dioxide 
emissions or CO2 emissions 
are emissions stemming 
from the burning of fossil 
fuels and the manufacture of 
cement; they include carbon 
dioxide produced during 
consumption of solid, liquid, 
and gas fuels as well as gas 
flaring. 
Carbon Dioxide Equivalent 
(CO2e): Carbon dioxide 
equivalent, or CO2e, is a 
metric measure representing 
all greenhouse gases by 
converting them to the 
equivalent amount of CO2. 
There are six greenhouse 
gases which contribute to 
the greenhouse effect, and 
thus to global warming, 06

Scope 1 Emissions: Scope 
1 emissions are direct 
greenhouse gas emissions 
occurring from the 
combustion of fuels owned 
or controlled by the reporting 
organisation. They are also 
called direct emissions.There are three recognised 
sources of Scope 1 
emissions: 
•   The burning of fossil fuels 
in a building or facility, e.g. 
a gas boiler or incinerator. 
This is the buildings’ heat 
and power submitted on 
the xtonnes platform. 
•   Refrigerants, or fugitive 
emissions, from cooling 
equipment such as fridges 
or air conditioners at 
installation, in use or in 
disposal. 
•   The burning of fossil fuels 
in mobile sources (on-road 
and non-road vehicles or 
mobile machinery). 
Scope 2 Emissions: 
Scope 2 emissions are the 
greenhouse gas emissions 
that result from a reporting 
organisation’s purchased 
energy, usually electricity, 
but also heating, cooling, 
and steam. It includes the 
emissions associated with 
the transport and distribution 
of this energy to a reporting 
organisation. They are also 
called indirect emissions. Scope 3 Emissions: Scope 
3 emissions are “indirect” 
emissions associated with 
a business's upstream and 
downstream supply chains. 
They are the remainder 
of greenhouse gas 
emissions that the reporting 
organisation must have to 
carry out its activities. The 
Greenhouse Gas Protocol 
has 15 categories of Scope 3 
emissions. 
Baseline Year: A baseline 
year is the year from which 
emission reduction targets 
are set, or benchmarked. 
The percentage of the total 
emissions in the base year 
sets future reduction targets. 
To meet Science Based 
Targets, the baseline year 
should be 2018, or for small 
businesses, the closest year 
for which there is good data.
